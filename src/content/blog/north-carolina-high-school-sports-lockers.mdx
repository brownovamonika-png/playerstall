## Why NC Humidity Is Your Locker Room's Biggest Enemy

This is the part most athletic directors don't hear until after they've made a mistake. North Carolina summers combine high temperatures — frequently above 90°F — with humidity levels that regularly exceed 70–80%. That combination is catastrophic for metal lockers.

Here's what happens. Sweat-soaked equipment comes off athletes and goes into metal lockers. The metal traps moisture. Cleaning chemicals accelerate oxidation. Within two to three years, you're seeing rust spots on the interior. Within five years, the finish is failing, hinges are corroding, and the room looks older than it is.

We've spoken with ADs in the Piedmont Triad and Charlotte metro who replaced their metal locker installations in under six years — spending the same budget twice when they could have invested in wood once.

Solid 3/4-inch birch construction behaves fundamentally differently. Wood's natural breathability allows moisture to move rather than concentrate. Sealed finishes protect the surface while ventilated door panels keep air circulating around equipment inside the locker. The result is a locker that stays structurally sound and visually clean decade after decade.

Coastal and eastern NC programs face an additional factor: salt air. The combination of coastal humidity and salt accelerates metal corrosion even faster. Wood is simply not susceptible to salt-driven oxidation.

Our lockers carry a lifetime guarantee — because we build them to actually last a lifetime, not because we expect to replace them.

## NCHSAA Compliance and Title IX Parity

NCHSAA member schools are expected to maintain athletic facilities that meet safety standards and provide an appropriate environment for student-athlete training and competition. While the NCHSAA doesn't prescribe locker room specifications in granular detail, schools operating under <a href="https://www.ada.gov" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ADA guidelines</a> and Title IX are required to meet federal standards on accessibility and gender equity.

Title IX is the piece most ADs underestimate. The law requires that female athletic programs receive facilities comparable in quality to male programs. This doesn't mean identical — it means equivalent in quality and condition. If your football program gets a full custom locker room upgrade, your women's soccer, basketball, or volleyball program needs a comparable facility investment.

This is actually an opportunity, not just a compliance burden. Planning locker room upgrades for both programs simultaneously allows you to negotiate better pricing on total order volume, maintain consistent design language across both facilities, and make a visible equity statement that resonates with parents, student-athletes, and school boards.

We work with programs on phased upgrades all the time. If budget requires sequencing, plan both facilities in the initial design phase and execute in two budget cycles.

## Funding Your NC Locker Room Upgrade

North Carolina athletic directors have several real funding pathways available for locker room improvements:

**Capital Improvement Allotments.** School districts receive capital improvement budgets annually. Locker room upgrades qualify as capital expenditures in most districts. The key is getting the project on the capital improvement plan before the budget cycle closes — typically in the spring for the following fiscal year.

**NC Education Lottery Proceeds.** North Carolina directs a portion of lottery proceeds to school capital projects through the Public School Building Capital Fund. Districts can apply these funds to athletic facility improvements. Contact your district's capital planning office to understand how these funds flow to your school.

**Local Bond Issues.** Many NC counties have passed school facility bonds in recent years. If your district has active bond funding, athletic facility upgrades are often eligible expenditures. Work with your principal and district administration to identify available funds.

**Booster Club Campaigns.** This is the most common path for high school programs. A targeted booster campaign with a specific dollar goal and visible outcome (a new locker room) motivates donor participation far better than general athletic fund appeals. A 40-locker installation at the Varsity tier ($449/locker) runs approximately $18,000. That's an achievable goal for a single campaign cycle at most NC programs.

Yes. Solid 3/4-inch birch construction with sealed finishes handles NC's heat and humidity far better than metal. A typical NC high school installation of 40–60 lockers runs $18,000–$35,000 depending on tier and customization. Programs commonly fund upgrades through capital improvement allotments, NC Education Lottery proceeds allocated at the district level, local bond issues, and booster club campaigns.
