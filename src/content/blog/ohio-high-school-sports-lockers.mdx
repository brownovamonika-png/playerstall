Football drives the most facility investment, but Ohio is genuinely multi-sport. Ohio is one of a handful of states where you'll see real facility budgets committed to all of these programs—not just football.

The competitive pressure extends beyond Ohio's borders. Programs in Columbus, Cleveland, and Cincinnati suburbs compete directly with schools in Kentucky, Pennsylvania, and Indiana for top regional recruits. A prospect who tours three similar programs in a weekend will remember the one with the professional locker room. ## Sport Priorities: What Ohio Programs Actually Need

### Football Is King—and It Shows in Equipment Volume

Ohio is one of America's premier football states. The OHSAA football playoffs are a regional spectacle, with Division I championship games drawing tens of thousands of fans to pro stadiums. Programs in communities like Massillon, Mentor, Gahanna Lincoln, and Pickerington North carry traditions that span generations.

Football also has the most demanding locker room requirements of any high school sport. A single varsity player's full kit includes two helmets, two sets of shoulder pads, home and away game jerseys, practice jerseys, multiple pairs of pants, cleats, undershirts, gloves, and personal gear. That's a volume problem. It doesn't fit in an 18" locker, and trying to make it fit creates daily frustration that coaches and players both resent.

Football lockers need to be wide. They need overhead helmet storage, a hanging rod for jersey organization, a deep lower shelf for shoulder pads, and a separate cleat compartment. Get those dimensions right and the locker room functions. Get them wrong and you've spent significant money on something that doesn't actually solve the problem.

### Basketball, Wrestling, and Baseball

Basketball is Ohio's winter signature sport. Programs in Dayton, Columbus, and Cleveland suburbs recruit aggressively at the high school level, and the locker room is consistently cited as a facility highlight during official visits. Basketball doesn't require the same locker width as football—18"–24" handles the equipment volume comfortably—but height matters, and customization for team branding has outsized impact in a sport where recruits are heavily recruited nationally.

Wrestling is one of Ohio's strongest sports nationally. The state produces more Division I wrestlers per capita than almost any other. Wrestling equipment is compact, but wrestling programs that share a locker room with football or basketball need flexible configurations. We discuss multi-sport locker room design in detail in our [multi-sport locker room design guide](/blog/multi-sport-locker-room-design).

Baseball programs across the state increasingly invest in facility quality as recruiting competition from the Southeast and West has grown. Custom wood lockers with bat storage and uniform hanging configurations give Ohio baseball programs a professional edge that metal installations can't match.

## Ohio's Climate Challenge for Locker Rooms

Ohio's weather is hard on buildings and harder on metal.

Summers in Ohio are humid. Columbus averages over 60% relative humidity from June through August, and locker rooms—especially those without robust HVAC—hold heat and moisture for hours after practice. Winters bring cold, wet conditions from Lake Erie across the northern part of the state and damp cold throughout the interior. That seasonal cycle—hot and humid, then cold and wet—is precisely the environment that makes metal lockers fail.

Metal lockers corrode at their base where moisture pools, pit at the bolt holes where hardware contacts the frame, and lose their painted finish along high-contact edges within a few years. Ohio programs that installed metal lockers in the early 2010s are dealing with rust-through and structural failure right now. Replacement cost for 60 metal lockers runs $10,000–$15,000—a cost that repeats every 8–12 years.

Solid 3/4" birch construction handles Ohio's climate differently. Wood is hygroscopic—it absorbs and releases moisture gradually rather than trapping it at contact points. Our lockers are sealed with high-quality finishes and include ventilation gaps in doors and panels that allow air circulation around equipment. The result is a locker that breathes with the environment rather than fighting it.

The analogy that works here: think about why traditional wood houses in Ohio outlast metal prefab structures. Wood accommodates the expansion and contraction of seasonal temperature changes in a way that metal doesn't. The same physics apply in the locker room.

## Sizing Guide for Ohio's Dominant Sports

### Football: Size for the Full Equipment Kit

Don't design for the equipment your players carry today—design for the equipment they'll carry at peak season with a full dress-out roster. Here are the sizing parameters we recommend for Ohio football programs:

**Width:** 24" minimum; 27"–30" preferred for varsity programs. Linemen and skill positions alike benefit from the wider configuration—equipment bulk varies, and a 24" locker that works in September gets tested hard by November when cold-weather layering adds volume.

**Height:** 76" standard. This gives you space for a fixed upper shelf (helmets), a hanging rod for jerseys, a lower shelf for shoulder pads, and a cleat compartment without stacking equipment on top of other equipment.

**Depth:** 24" is the standard and handles football gear well.

**Interior configuration:** Fixed upper shelf, single hanging rod, lower shelf for shoulder pads, separate floor compartment for cleats and footwear. Programs that add a lockable personal storage box inside the locker (for phones, wallets, and keys) reduce equipment room theft and locker room culture issues.

### Basketball and Baseball: 18"–24" Is the Sweet Spot

For basketball programs, an 18"–24" wide locker with proper hanging rod configuration and a shoe shelf handles the equipment volume well. The game jersey, practice jersey, shorts, warmup suit, and two or three pairs of shoes fit without crowding in a 20"–24" locker.

Baseball lockers need specific attention to vertical bat storage. A 24"–27" locker with a dedicated bat column on one side, a hanging rod for uniforms, and a helmet shelf at the top works for most Ohio high school programs. College-bound programs often prefer 27"–30" to give players the full professional feel.

## Funding Your Locker Room Upgrade in Ohio

Ohio districts have more funding mechanisms available than most ADs realize. The challenge is matching the right mechanism to the right project size.

**Capital improvement funds** are the starting point. Most Ohio districts maintain a capital improvement budget line for facility maintenance and upgrades. For projects under $40,000, capital improvement funds are often the fastest path to approval because they don't require a voter referendum. The key is framing the locker room upgrade as facility infrastructure—not a luxury purchase. Document the current locker room's condition with photos, estimate deferred maintenance costs on the existing installation, and present the total cost of ownership comparison between continued repairs and a new installation.

**Bond issues** handle larger projects. Ohio districts put facility bond issues on the ballot regularly, and athletic facility upgrades are a reliable component of broader bond packages. If your district has a bond measure coming up, lobbying to include the locker room renovation is far easier than mounting a standalone campaign. The typical Ohio school facility bond is a 20–25 year instrument at modest interest rates, making the annual cost of a $60,000 locker room installation roughly $3,500–$4,500 per year—a number that's easy to justify against recruiting ROI.

**School levies** in Ohio sometimes include capital provisions that can be directed toward facility upgrades. Work with your district's treasurer to understand what flexibility exists in your levy structure.

**Booster clubs and athletic foundations** fill the gap. Ohio has some of the most active boosters organizations in high school athletics. A naming rights opportunity attached to a locker room renovation—the "XYZ Family Football Locker Room"—regularly generates $15,000–$50,000 in booster contributions that offset public funding requirements.

### What to Budget

A 50–60 locker installation for a typical Ohio high school program—Varsity tier, two sports (football primary), standard customization—runs $35,000–$55,000 including shipping and installation. That covers the lockers, delivery to your facility, and professional installation by a crew familiar with athletic locker room projects.

Larger programs (80–100 lockers), premium tiers, or full locker room renovations that include electrical, flooring, and ventilation work push to $70,000–$120,000. Those broader projects require coordination with additional trades, and we're experienced at sequencing our installation within a larger construction schedule.

The cheapest part of the project is almost never the lockers themselves. Getting the space right—proper ventilation, adequate lighting, ADA-compliant aisles and accessible units—matters as much as locker quality for the long-term success of the renovation.
