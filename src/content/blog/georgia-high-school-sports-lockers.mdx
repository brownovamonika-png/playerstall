## Georgia's Humidity Problem—and the Right Solution

Georgia's climate is one of the most demanding in the country for locker room materials. Here's what that means in practical terms.

Atlanta averages over 70% relative humidity from May through September. Savannah, Augusta, and southern Georgia run higher—80%+ during summer months is common. That humidity doesn't stay outside. It moves into locker rooms through ventilation systems, on wet equipment, and on players' gear after outdoor practice in August heat. The locker room environment in a Georgia high school in August is genuinely humid.

Metal lockers fail in this environment systematically. Rust forms at the base where moisture pools on the floor. Corrosion starts at hardware contact points—bolts, hinges, handle mounting holes—and spreads outward. The painted finish on metal lockers blisters and flakes under repeated moisture-and-dry cycles, leaving exposed metal that corrodes faster. Georgia programs that installed metal lockers in the early 2010s are dealing with structural failure right now. The programs that installed them in the early 2000s are on their second or third replacement cycle.

The math is damaging: a 60-locker metal installation at $200 per unit costs $12,000 initially. With 8–10 year replacement cycles, you've spent $24,000–$36,000 over 20 years on lockers that looked increasingly bad for the second half of each cycle.

Solid 3/4" birch handles Georgia's humidity cycle differently. Wood is hygroscopic—it absorbs and releases moisture gradually rather than trapping it at corrosion-prone contact points. Our lockers are sealed with high-quality finishes that protect the surface and include ventilation gaps in doors and side panels that allow air to move through the locker rather than pool against equipment. The result is a locker that still looks professional in year 15 and doesn't need replacing before year 20.

Think of it this way: wood houses in Georgia's coastal areas—built and maintained properly—last generations. Metal sheds in the same climate rust through in a decade. The same principle applies at the locker scale.

## SPLOST and Other Funding Mechanisms in Georgia

Georgia has a funding mechanism that most other states don't: SPLOST, and understanding it can materially change how you approach your locker room project.

### What SPLOST Is and How It Works

Special Purpose Local Option Sales Tax is a Georgia-specific instrument that allows county voters to approve a temporary 1% sales tax increase for specific capital projects. Unlike operating budget funds (which cover salaries, supplies, and maintenance) and unlike general obligation bonds (which require debt service over 20+ years), SPLOST money is purpose-restricted capital that can only be spent on the projects voters approve.

Athletic facility improvements—including locker rooms, weight rooms, and field upgrades—are a common and popular SPLOST category. Georgia counties have used SPLOST rounds to fund complete athletic facility overhauls, including full locker room renovations, that would otherwise require multi-year bond campaigns or general fund raids.

### How to Position Your Project for SPLOST

If your county has a SPLOST round approaching, the window to get athletic facilities included in the project list is before the ballot language is finalized. That typically means working with your superintendent and district facilities office 12–18 months before the expected vote. Athletic facility renovations that include locker rooms, accessibility upgrades, and energy-efficient equipment tend to perform well with voters—they're visible, tangible improvements that parents and community members understand immediately.

If your county's SPLOST cycle is several years out, other mechanisms include capital improvement funds from the district's operating budget (best for projects under $30,000), bond issues for larger comprehensive renovations, and booster club campaigns that can cover $15,000–$50,000 in contributions toward a named facility. The "XYZ Family Football Locker Room" naming opportunity is a reliable donor retention vehicle at the right program and budget level.

## Sizing Guide for Georgia's Major Sports

### Football: Size for the Full Kit

Georgia football programs should plan for 24"–30" wide lockers as the minimum. The equipment volume is the same as Ohio or Texas—two helmets, multiple sets of shoulder pads, home and away uniforms, multiple footwear pairs, and personal gear—and it doesn't fit in an 18" locker.

Here are the specs we recommend for Georgia football programs:

**Width:** 24" minimum for shared-sport rooms; 27"–30" preferred for dedicated football installations. Offensive and defensive linemen carry more equipment bulk than skill positions, and planning to the upper end of the range eliminates the configuration problems that underpowered lockers create.

**Height:** 76" standard. Overhead helmet shelf, hanging rod for uniforms, lower pad shelf, and a cleat compartment should all be included in the interior configuration.

**Depth:** 24" standard handles football equipment well.

Class 7A programs in metro Atlanta competing for D1 recruits should consider Pro ($549) or Stadium ($599) tier for the finish quality and customization options that match what recruits see on campus visits.

### Basketball, Baseball, and Soccer

Basketball programs in Georgia's competitive suburban counties—Gwinnett, DeKalb, Cobb—recruit hard and tour facilities as part of that process. An 18"–24" wide locker handles basketball equipment well; the investment is in finish quality, team branding, and professional appearance.

Georgia baseball programs need attention to bat storage—a dedicated vertical bat column in a 24"–27" locker, alongside a helmet shelf and uniform hanging space. Soccer programs at 18"–20" width with ventilated doors (cleats and shin guards need to dry) work well for most programs.

## Typical Project Costs and Timelines

### Budget Framework for Georgia Programs

A 50–60 locker installation for a typical Georgia GHSA Class 5A program—Varsity tier, football primary, standard team color customization—runs $32,000–$52,000 including shipping and installation. Class 6A and 7A programs going Pro or Stadium tier push to $45,000–$70,000 for similar locker counts.

Programs doing full facility renovations—new flooring, ventilation upgrades, lighting, ADA accessibility retrofits alongside new lockers—run $70,000–$150,000 and require coordination with additional contractors. The locker installation happens after floor, electrical, and ventilation work is complete.

Georgia high school athletic facilities must meet ADA accessibility standards. <a href="https://www.ada.gov/resources/2010-ada-standards/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ADA's 2010 standards</a> require at least one accessible locker per 25 standard lockers, 36" minimum aisle widths, and clear floor space of 30" × 48" in front of accessible units. Build these requirements into your design from day one—retrofitting after installation costs more and looks worse.

### Timeline for Georgia Programs

Georgia ADs targeting summer installation should start the planning process in January or February. Two-a-days in Georgia run August, and you want the locker room complete before that first day of fall camp. Working backwards from an August 1 target:

- **February:** Initial consultation, space measurement, design review
- **March:** Board or SPLOST documentation, order approval
- **April:** Order placed; fabrication begins (6–10 week lead time)
- **June:** Lockers arrive; installation begins (3–5 days on-site)
- **July:** Complete, tested, and ready for fall

Programs that contact us in May for a July delivery are working in a tight window. Programs that contact us in February have the timeline working for them, not against them.
