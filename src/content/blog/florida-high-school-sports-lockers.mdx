## The Florida High School Athletics Landscape

Florida is a heavyweight in American high school athletics. The <a href="https://www.fhsaa.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Florida High School Athletic Association (FHSAA)</a> governs more than 800 member schools and hundreds of thousands of student-athletes across 7 classifications. No other state association combines that sheer scale with Florida's unique environmental and demographic intensity.

Athletics in Florida operates year-round. Football camps begin in July under brutal heat. Basketball runs fall through winter. Baseball and soccer overlap in the spring. Track and field, lacrosse, and swimming fill the gaps. There is no off-season for the locker room. Facilities get used 10–11 months per year by multiple sports cycling through the same spaces.

That constant usage multiplies the wear on every surface. It also multiplies the ROI on a quality upgrade. A locker room that serves three or four sports programs simultaneously is infrastructure, not a luxury for one team.

Florida also produces an outsized share of national D1 athletic talent. Programs in Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach, Hillsborough, and Orange counties are known quantities to recruiting coordinators across the SEC, ACC, and Big 12. High school programs in these counties compete not just against each other — they compete against college programs for the attention and commitment of the state's top athletes.

The locker room is one of the few facility investments a high school program fully controls. Use it.

## Why Florida's Climate Destroys Metal Lockers

Let's be specific about what happens to metal lockers in Florida. It isn't abstract corrosion. It's a predictable sequence that costs programs thousands of dollars in replacement cycles.

Year one through two: the locker looks fine. Small rust spots appear inside where equipment contacts metal surfaces. Hinges begin stiffening slightly.

Year three through four: rust stains are visible on interior surfaces. The paint finish on exterior doors starts chalking and peeling in high-traffic areas. Cleaning chemicals — which most programs use weekly — accelerate oxidation on unprotected metal edges.

Year five through seven: hinge failure becomes common. Doors don't close properly. Rust has spread to structural surfaces. The room looks dated, regardless of how clean the floor is.

Coastal programs have it worse. Schools in Miami Beach, Fort Lauderdale, Naples, Sarasota, and along both coasts deal with airborne salt that works into every crevice. Salt air doesn't care about paint finish — it finds the seams and starts working. Metal locker replacement in coastal Florida schools happens in 5 years or less in many cases.

We've spoken with ADs who had gone through two full metal locker installations at the same school within ten years — spending the same capital budget twice. That math doesn't work.

Solid 3/4-inch birch construction with sealed finishes simply doesn't behave this way. Wood doesn't rust. Wood doesn't corrode from salt air. Wood's natural structure allows moisture to move through and exit rather than concentrate at a corrosion point.

## Wood's Humidity Advantage: What the Science Says

The key isn't just that wood doesn't rust. It's that solid wood construction manages moisture actively, while metal merely resists it until the resistance fails.

Properly sealed birch plywood — 3/4-inch thickness throughout — provides dimensional stability under Florida's humidity cycling. When humidity rises, the wood absorbs marginally and redistributes. When the HVAC drops humidity in the locker room, it releases. This breathing behavior is why wood furniture lasts for generations in humid climates while cheap metal furniture corrodes.

Ventilated door panels are the complement to this. Our locker doors include ventilation gaps that allow air circulation inside the locker itself. Equipment — sweaty football pads, wet cleats, damp baseball uniforms — benefits from airflow that accelerates drying. Metal lockers with solid doors trap moisture around equipment, creating the bacterial conditions that cause both odor and equipment degradation.

Think of it like the difference between storing food in a breathable wooden crate versus sealing it in a metal container. One manages moisture, the other traps it.

The practical outcome for a Florida program: lockers that look as good in year fifteen as they did in year one, equipment that dries faster between uses, and an end to the rust-and-replace cycle.

## Funding a Florida Locker Room Upgrade

Florida athletic directors have several real funding pathways:

**PECO (Public Education Capital Outlay).** Florida's PECO program funds school facility construction and renovation projects, including athletic facilities. Projects are prioritized through district capital plans. Connect with your district's facilities department to understand how PECO funds flow to school-level projects and what the application timeline looks like.

**Local Bond Referendums.** Many Florida counties have passed school facility bonds in recent cycles. Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach, Orange, and Hillsborough counties have all had recent bond measures that include athletic facility improvements as eligible expenditures.

**Booster Club Campaigns.** A focused booster campaign with a specific, visible goal is the most common path for high school programs. A 45-locker installation at the Pro tier ($549/locker) runs approximately $24,705. That's a real number for a single capital campaign at a program with an engaged booster base.

**Athletic Department Capital Budgets.** Some programs accumulate capital budget surpluses that can be deployed for facility upgrades in a single cycle. Check with your principal and district athletic coordinator about what's allowable.

**Phased Approach.** If full funding isn't available in one cycle, plan the complete design and execute in two phases. Phase 1 might cover the primary football locker room. Phase 2, funded the following year, covers the women's basketball or soccer facility. We work with programs on phased installations regularly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long do metal lockers last in Florida's climate?

In high-humidity Florida environments — especially coastal schools with salt air — metal lockers typically show significant rust and finish degradation within 5–7 years. Coastal schools in Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach, and along both coasts often see failure even faster. Solid wood lockers with sealed finishes last 20+ years in the same conditions.

### What makes wood lockers better for Florida's humidity?

Solid 3/4-inch birch construction with sealed finishes and ventilated door panels allows moisture to circulate rather than concentrate. Wood doesn't rust, doesn't corrode from salt air, and doesn't trap sweat the way metal does. Proper ventilation in locker design extends life further by preventing moisture buildup inside the locker.

### How much does a Florida high school locker room upgrade cost?

A typical FHSAA-school installation of 40–60 lockers runs $18,000–$35,000 depending on tier and customization. Common funding sources include PECO (Public Education Capital Outlay) funds, local school district bond referendums, booster club campaigns, and athletic department capital budgets.

### What FHSAA or ADA compliance requirements apply to locker rooms?

FHSAA member schools must meet <a href="https://www.ada.gov" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ADA</a> accessibility standards for athletic facilities, including locker rooms. Title IX requires comparable facility quality for male and female athletic programs. We design locker rooms that meet both requirements and can help you plan equitable upgrades for all programs.

### What locker tier is best for a Florida 7A or 6A program?

Competitive 6A and 7A programs typically choose the Pro ($549) or Stadium ($599) tier. These programs attract D1 talent and compete for recruits nationally — the facility needs to match that level. Smaller programs and first-time upgraders often start with the Varsity ($449) tier.

### How early should Florida ADs plan a locker room project?

Plan 10–14 weeks from contract to installation. Florida's year-round sports calendar means finding an installation window requires planning. Most programs target June–July between spring and fall seasons. Order by early April to secure a summer slot.
