**Topic:** Hockey program case study — "The first thing the parents noticed was the smell"

The first thing the parents noticed wasn't the new lockers. It was the smell.

A hockey coach called me last spring. His program had been dressing in the same locker room for 20 years: metal lockers, dented doors, gear that never fully dried between practices. Every season, the same complaints. Wet equipment sat in sealed steel boxes overnight. Players showed up to morning skates pulling on damp gloves.

He told me he'd stopped taking recruits' families through the dressing room. He walked them around it.

We built him a set of wood lockers with open ventilation, a gear shelf sized for hockey bags, a stick rack, and the team colours and crest on every door. He didn't ask for anything fancy. He asked for a room he wasn't embarrassed to show people.

A few weeks into the season, he called me again. He said three things had changed:

1. Gear was actually dry by morning.
2. The room stopped smelling like a hockey bag.
3. A recruit's dad stood in the doorway, looked around, and said, "This is a real program."

That last one is the one I think about. Nobody chooses a school because of a locker. But families read the details. A room that's cared for tells them the program cares about its players.

I've had some version of that call many times in 30+ years. It never gets old.

If you coach or run a program: what's the first thing a visitor sees when they walk into your locker room?
