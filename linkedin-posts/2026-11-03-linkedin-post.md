Curt's Quick Tip: The best lockers in the world will still frustrate your team if the room is laid out wrong.

I've walked into a lot of beautiful locker rooms in 30+ years where the lockers were perfect and the room was a mess. Twenty athletes trying to get through one gap. A bench blocking the door swing. Everyone squeezed into the same corner five minutes before warm-up.

Almost every time, the lockers weren't the problem. The layout was.

Here's the tip: before you pick a single finish or colour, walk the room like a player would.

- Where does everyone come in, and where do they all need to end up? Leave a real aisle there, not a polite one.
- Does every locker door and bench have room to open fully while someone else walks past?
- Are your biggest athletes (linemen, goalies) at the ends or corners, where they have the most elbow room, not wedged in the middle?
- Is there somewhere to dry gear that isn't the middle of the walkway?

Sketch it on paper first. Put an X where the crowd forms at 3:30 on a practice day. Then fix that spot before anything gets built.

Because our lockers are modular, we can adjust a layout late in the process, add a corner unit or swap a bay for a wider one, without starting over. But it only works if someone asks the traffic question early.

A good locker room feels easy. Nobody notices the layout when it's right.
