Curt's Quick Tip: Before you order a single locker, measure the equipment — not the room.

I've seen this mistake more times than I can count in 30+ years.

A program picks a locker size off a catalogue, based on how many bays fit along the wall. Looks great on paper. Then preseason hits, the shoulder pads, helmets, bags and cleats come out, and suddenly every athlete is cramming gear into a space that was never built for it. Doors won't close. Equipment gets damp. Kids stop using the locker and start living out of their bags.

Here's the tip: grab the bulkiest kit your athletes actually carry and measure it.

- Football? Helmet, shoulder pads, and game-day jersey hanging full length.
- Hockey? Skates, pants, and a bag that has to breathe.
- Basketball? Shoes, warm-ups, and a street-clothes change.

Then work backwards. Pick the locker width, height and depth that fits that gear comfortably, and fit the layout around it. If the room only holds 40 bays at that size, you either build a smarter layout or phase the install. You don't shrink the locker.

A locker that's too small never gets fixed later. It just gets resented every single day.

Because our lockers are modular, we can mix sizes in one room: deeper units for the linemen, standard units for skill positions. But it all starts with someone with a tape measure and an honest look at the gear.
