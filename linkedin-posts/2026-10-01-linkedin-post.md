"Ask Curt" — Can I just wipe down wood lockers like I would metal ones?

Almost. But the one thing people get wrong is the thing that causes the most damage: too much water.

I get this question a lot, usually right after a facilities manager realizes their new lockers aren't going to need repainting every few years. Wood is forgiving, but it does have a few rules. After 30+ years of building these, here's the short version of what I tell everyone.

Use a soft cloth that's damp, not wet. Warm water and a drop of mild soap handles 95% of what a locker room throws at it. Wipe, then follow with a dry cloth so moisture doesn't sit in the corners or along the base.

Skip the harsh stuff. Bleach, abrasive pads, and heavy solvents will dull the finish and can break it down over time. If you need to disinfect, use a product that's rated for finished wood and test it on an inside panel first.

Let it breathe. This is the part people overlook. Wood naturally manages moisture, so don't seal wet gear in there for days. Leave doors open when the room is empty for a long weekend, and good ventilation does half your cleaning for you.

Catch small things early. A loose hinge screw or a chipped corner takes two minutes to fix today and a lot longer if it's ignored for a season. And because our lockers are modular, if a piece ever does need replacing, we send that piece, not a whole new locker.

That's it. No special equipment, no secret product. A little care a few times a season, and a wood locker room just keeps looking like it did the week it was installed.
