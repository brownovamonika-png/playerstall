**Pillar:** Behind-the-Scenes
**Topic:** Quality control — every locker checked before it ships

---

Before any locker leaves our shop, someone opens and closes every single door. Twice.

It sounds almost too simple to mention. But it's the reason I rarely get the phone call I dread most.

Here's the thing about a locker room: nobody thinks about the lockers until one of them fails. A door that sticks. A hinge that sags. A shelf that doesn't sit level. By the time you notice, the lockers are installed, the team is moving in, and the fix means a delay you never planned for.

So we catch it in the shop instead. Doors swing free. Hinges are checked. Shelves, hooks, and hardware are tested. Finish is inspected in real light, not just glanced at. Every piece gets looked at by someone who knows exactly what it should feel like, because they've built hundreds of them.

Is it slower than boxing things up and shipping? Absolutely. After 30+ years, I've learned that a day spent in the shop saves a week of headaches on your end, and I'd much rather be the one who finds the problem.

It's also the only way I'm comfortable standing behind a lifetime guarantee. If we're going to promise these lockers will last, we'd better be sure they're right the day they leave our hands.

Athletic directors and facilities managers: when you're comparing suppliers, do you ask what happens before the product ships? What do you wish more vendors would check?
