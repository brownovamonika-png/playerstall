The cheaper locker isn't actually cheaper. Let me show you the math.

Every year I talk to athletic directors comparing quotes, and metal almost always looks like the smart budget choice on paper. Lower sticker price, faster lead time, done deal. Then I ask one question: "How long do you expect that locker room to last?"

Most of them haven't run the numbers past year one.

Here's what 30 years of building these things has taught me. A quality metal locker holds up for 8 to 10 years before rust, dents, and hinge failures start piling up. A solid wood locker, built right, is still going strong at 15, 20, sometimes 30 years. I've got lockers from 1996 still in daily use right now.

So do the actual cost-per-year math. A $400 metal locker that needs replacing at year 9 costs you roughly $44/year — and that's before you factor in the second install, the second disruption to your season, the second round of shipping and labor. A $600 wood locker that lasts 20 years runs about $30/year. Wood wins, and it's not close.

That's before you even get to the stuff that's harder to put a number on: no rust stains on jerseys, no dents that make a locker room look tired after year three, no odor building up in a sealed metal box. Just a locker room that still looks like it belongs to a program that takes itself seriously.

Lower upfront cost isn't the same as lower cost. When you're comparing quotes, what timeframe are you actually planning around — one budget cycle, or one program?
