A coach called me last week. Three lockers in his room had taken a beating — one door was cracked, a bench seat was splitting, hinges were shot. He asked how much it would cost to replace the whole locker room.

My answer surprised him: it wouldn't cost anything close to that, because he didn't need to.

Here's something most athletic directors don't find out until it's too late: with most metal lockers, damage to one section usually means replacing the whole unit. The frame, the doors, the shelving — it's all one welded piece. Break a hinge on one end, and you're often looking at a full teardown just to fix it.

We build differently. Every PlayerStall locker is modular by design — individual components that can be repaired or swapped out on their own. A cracked door gets replaced. A worn bench gets replaced. The rest of the locker, and the rest of the room, stays exactly as it was.

That's not just a manufacturing choice. It's a cost decision we made 30 years ago, because we were tired of watching programs get quoted for full replacements over damage to a single part.

It's also why our lockers last 15+ years instead of 8-10. You're not managing a ticking clock on the whole system — you're maintaining pieces as needed, for decades.

If your locker room has a few beat-up spots right now, I'd bet money you don't need a new room. You need new parts.
