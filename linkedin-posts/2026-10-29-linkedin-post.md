"Ask Curt" — How long do wood lockers actually last?

I'll give you the honest answer, not the brochure answer: it depends far more on the room than on the wood.

I've been asked this by athletic directors, facilities managers and school board members for over 30 years, usually when they're staring at two quotes and trying to justify the higher one. So here's what I've seen.

In a dry, ventilated room with normal care, wood lockers regularly go 15 years and well beyond. We have programs running lockers we built decades ago that still look sharp. Metal in the same conditions tends to show its age in 8 to 10 years: dents, rust at the base, doors that no longer close right.

What actually shortens a locker's life? Three things. Trapped moisture from wet gear sealed in for days. Constant abuse of hinges and latches that never get a tightened screw. And rooms with no airflow at all.

What extends it? Wood that breathes, a quick wipe-down a few times a season, and a modular design. If one door or shelf takes a beating, you replace that piece, not the whole locker. That's a big reason our lifetime guarantee is something I can say out loud without flinching.

So when someone asks "how long will it last," I flip it around: how long do you plan to have this program? Buy for that number.
