Curt's Quick Tip: If your locker doesn't breathe, your equipment is rotting while you sleep.

I've walked into locker rooms in 30+ years that looked fantastic and smelled like a swamp. Nine times out of ten, the problem wasn't the cleaning crew. It was airflow.

Think about what goes into a locker after a hard practice: sweaty pads, damp jerseys, wet gloves, a pair of skates still dripping. Close the door on that and you've built a tiny, sealed greenhouse. Moisture has nowhere to go, so it sits there and grows odour, mold and mildew. Then it spreads to the next locker, and the next.

Here's the tip: before you buy, look at how the locker actually moves air.

- Are there vents or slotted doors, and are they in the right places (top and bottom, so air really circulates)?
- Is there room between the gear and the walls, or is everything jammed tight?
- Does the material itself help? Wood naturally handles moisture better than sealed metal, which is a big part of why I build with it.

And a habit worth teaching your athletes: hang gear, don't ball it up. Ten seconds of effort at the end of practice saves months of that smell.

Good ventilation isn't a luxury feature. It's what keeps equipment lasting longer, locker rooms healthier, and players actually wanting to use their space.

What's the worst locker room smell you've ever walked into? I'll bet ventilation was part of the story.
