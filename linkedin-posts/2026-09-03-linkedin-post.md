Same room. Same team. Completely different energy.

Here's what changed.

A hockey program reached out to us last year. Their locker room hadn't been touched since the building went up in the 90s — dented metal lockers, mismatched paint, a couple of doors that didn't even close right anymore. The head coach told me, "Curt, our players get changed in here for two hours a day. It shouldn't feel like an afterthought."

We measured the space, mapped out a modular layout that used every inch efficiently, and built a full set of custom wood lockers in the team's colors — logo engraved above every stall, player names on the nameplates.

When the install crew finished and the team walked in for the first time, the coach sent me a photo. Same four walls. Same square footage. But the room looked — and felt — like a completely different program. One that took itself seriously. One players wanted to be part of.

That's the part people underestimate about a locker room upgrade. It's not just storage. It's the first impression your program makes on every player, every parent, every recruit who walks through that door.

You don't need to gut the building to change how a space feels. Sometimes it's just the lockers.
