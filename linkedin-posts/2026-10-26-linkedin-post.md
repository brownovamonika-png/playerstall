The most important step in building a locker happens after it looks finished. It's the part nobody sees, and it's the part your athletes feel every day.

I spend a lot of time in our finishing area, and I'll tell you what I tell every new person who joins us: a locker is not done when the last screw goes in.

It's done when someone runs a hand along every edge, every door and every shelf, and nothing catches. No rough corner that snags a jersey. No splinter waiting for a hockey glove. No sharp edge a 14-year-old will find with his knuckles at 6 a.m.

That's hand work. You can't shortcut it, and you can't fake it. A team's colors only look right when the surface underneath is right, and a finish only lasts 15+ years if it's put on properly the first time.

When we match a program's colors, we check them in the real light of a real room, not just against a swatch. Colors that look perfect under shop lights can look completely different in a locker room, and I'd rather catch that here than have a coach catch it on install day.

After 30+ years, I've learned that quality isn't one big decision. It's a few hundred small ones nobody will ever thank you for. But your athletes feel every one of them, even if they never know why the room just feels right.

If you've ever walked into a locker room and thought "someone really cared about this place," that's what I'm talking about.
